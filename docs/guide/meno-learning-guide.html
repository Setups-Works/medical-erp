CHAPTER 01

The pharmacy business

Medicines travel from the company that makes them to the patient through a few hands. Each step adds a margin and a bill.

Manufacturer→C&F agent / stockist→Distributor (wholesale)→Pharmacy (retail)→Patient
WhoBuys atSells atPays
DistributorPTS (price to stockist), without GSTPTR (price to retailer), without GSTUsually on credit, 15–45 days
PharmacyPTR + GSTMRP or less (GST already inside MRP)Patients pay cash, UPI or card; some regulars on credit
PatientMRP or less—On the spot

The five things that must always be right

PriceNever more than the MRP printed on the pack. Discounts within limits.
TaxGST at the right rate for the medicine, split correctly by state.
Stock by batchWhich batch, how many, when it expires.
Legal recordsPrescriptions and registers for restricted medicines.
Money owedWho owes whom, how much, since when.

Two editions of MENO

Retail is for the pharmacy counter: fast billing, offline desktop app, prescriptions. Wholesale is for distributors: orders, credit, schemes, dispatch, e-invoice. Masters, stock, GST, accounts and reports are shared.

CHAPTER 02

Ten key ideas

Learn these ten and the rest of MENO makes sense.

  1. MRP already includes GST. At the counter MENO works backwards: price ÷ (1 + GST%) = taxable value; the rest is GST.
  2. PTR does not include GST. Wholesale bills add GST on top of the PTR.
  3. Every medicine is sold by batch. A batch has a number, an expiry month and its own MRP and cost.
  4. First expiry, first out (FEFO). MENO picks the batch that expires first. Expired batches can never be sold.
  5. The GST rate comes from the HSN code and the date. Nobody types the rate on a bill. Most medicines are 5 % from 22 Sept 2025, some are Nil, some 18 %.
  6. Same state → CGST + SGST. Different state → IGST. The total tax is the same; it is split differently.
  7. Restricted medicines need a prescription. Schedule H, H1, X and narcotics. H1 sales are written into a legal register.
  8. A saved (posted) bill never changes. Mistakes are fixed with a new document: a credit note, a debit note or a reversal.
  9. Stock is a list of movements. Every in and out is a row. The stock number is just the sum, so it can always be checked.
  10. Every bill creates accounting entries by itself. Debits equal credits, always. Nobody types the books.

CHAPTER 04

Setting up a shop

Done once, in about 30 minutes for a small shop. MENO saves every step, so you can stop and come back.

  1. Sign up and verify your mobile (OTP) and email.
  2. Business profile: retail, wholesale or both; how many branches; which software you use today.
  3. Company and GST: type the GSTIN and press Fetch. Name, address and state fill in. The state is locked to the GSTIN, because a wrong state is the most common GST mistake.
  4. Licences and pharmacist: drug licence numbers with expiry, and the pharmacist in charge.
  5. Branches and counters, then books: financial year (April–March), bank accounts, opening balances. Debits must equal credits.
  6. Billing preferences: bill number pattern, rounding, discount limits, invoice look with a live preview.
  7. Team: invite staff with roles.
  8. Data: start fresh, import Excel, or move data from Marg, Tally, Busy or Vyapar. MENO shows errors first and imports only after you approve.
  9. Devices: install the desktop app, test the printer and scanner.
  10. Practice on a sample company, then go live. MENO checks everything is ready first.

CHAPTER 06

Buying stock

A supplier sends a bill. You enter it (or import it). Stock goes up by batch, and you now owe the supplier.

Supplier bill arrives→Enter or import→MENO checks→Totals match the paper bill→Post→Stock in + supplier owed + input GST

What MENO checks

  • The same supplier bill number cannot be entered twice.
  • Expired batches are blocked; near-expiry ones warn.
  • Rates or MRP different from last time are highlighted.
  • Your total must match the supplier's printed total within ₹1.

Worked example: free goods

Purchase line (PTR excludes GST)
Paracip 650, 10 strips × ₹24.00240.00
Free1 strip
Taxable value240.00
GST 5 % (CGST 6.00 + SGST 6.00)12.00
Line total₹252.00

Stock goes up by 11 strips, not 10. The free strip makes each strip cheaper.

Landed cost: what one unit really cost

Landed cost = (taxable value + share of freight + any GST you cannot claim back) ÷ (billed + free units).

CHAPTER 07

Selling at the counter

The busiest screen. Scan, check, take payment, print. It must be fast and must never break a rule.

Scan or search (F2)→MENO picks first-expiry batch→Checks→Payment (F9)→Save and print (F10)

Checks on every line

  • Expired or banned medicine → blocked.
  • Schedule H / H1 / X → doctor and prescription before saving (F4).
  • Allergy listed for this patient → warning the pharmacist must acknowledge.
  • Discount above your limit → manager PIN.
  • Price above MRP → never allowed.

Loose tablets

Price of one tablet = MRP of the strip ÷ tablets in the strip. Stock goes down by a part of a strip.

Loose sale calculator

Payment

Cash, UPI, card, credit, or a mix. MENO shows change for cash. Credit is only for a saved customer within their limit. Pressing F10 twice still makes one bill.

No internet?

The desktop app keeps billing with the same maths and sends the bills later. Each offline bill is signed by the counter PC so it cannot be faked.

CHAPTER 08

Restricted medicines and registers

Some medicines can only be sold on a doctor's prescription. The law also asks for records.

GroupRuleWhat MENO does
OTCNo prescription neededNormal billing
Schedule HPrescription requiredBlocks saving until doctor and prescription are entered
Schedule H1Prescription + separate register: prescriber name and address, patient name, medicine, quantity; kept 3 yearsWrites the register automatically from the bill
Schedule XStricter records and prescription copySeparate register (to confirm with pharmacist)
Narcotics (NDPS)Separate state licenceBlocked in the pilot until set up
Banned combinationsNever sellBlocked at purchase and sale
A registered pharmacist must supervise the sale of prescription medicines. Expired stock must be kept apart, marked not for sale, and returned or destroyed properly.

Price control: for essential medicines the government sets a ceiling price. You can never charge more than the lower of the price-list price and the label price.

CHAPTER 09

Selling to pharmacies (wholesale)

A distributor sells to many pharmacies on credit. The day runs from orders to collections.

Order (rep app, phone, portal)→Checks: licence, credit→Invoice at PTR + schemes→Dispatch check by scan→e-invoice / e-way bill→Delivery→Collection by due date

Terms you will see

  • Credit days: due date = bill date + credit days.
  • Schemes: "10 + 1" free goods, scheme %, trade discount, cash discount. On the bill they all lower the taxable value before GST.
  • E-invoice: needed when yearly turnover is above ₹5 crore. E-way bill: needed when goods worth more than ₹50,000 move.

Worked example: one printed bill with returns

A distributor's bill can show sales, minus returns, minus broken or expired goods, plus replacements, plus freight. Underneath, each return is a separate credit note.

CHAPTER 10

Returns, expiry and claims

SituationWhat happens in MENO
A patient returns a stripSales return against the original bill: same batch, same price and GST. Stock goes back only if it passes inspection. Refund or credit note.
Stock is near expiryShown at 30 / 60 / 90 days. Return to supplier, discount it, or move it to a branch where it sells.
Stock expired or brokenKept apart. Returned to the supplier as a claim; the supplier's credit note settles it. Or destroyed with a record.
A wrong rate on an old billA money-only credit or debit note. No stock changes.
A return with no billOff by default. If the shop allows it: only sealed, unexpired, non-prescription items, with manager approval.

CHAPTER 11

Money: who owes whom

Every supplier and customer has an account (a ledger). Bills add to it; payments take from it.

Matching a payment to bills

MENO pays the oldest bills first unless you choose. Extra money stays "on account".

Customer pays ₹650
Bill A (oldest)500.00 paid
Bill B150.00 of 300.00 paid
Left on account₹0.00

Credit control

Before a credit sale MENO adds: what they owe now + unposted orders + this bill. If that is above the limit, the owner must approve.

Interest on late payment

Simple interest = amount × rate % × days ÷ 365. GST also applies to interest you charge for late payment.

Interest calculator

Tax deducted from payments (TDS)

Big buyers (turnover above ₹10 crore) keep back 0.1 % when their purchases from one seller cross ₹50 lakh in a year. MENO reads these numbers from settings, because the law changes (the new Income Tax Act applies from 1 April 2026).

Paying small suppliers on time

Micro and small (MSME) suppliers must be paid within 15 days, or up to 45 days with a written agreement. Otherwise the expense is only allowed for income tax when it is paid.

CHAPTER 12

Accounts happen by themselves

Every saved bill creates a voucher with equal debits and credits. From vouchers come all the books.

Debit and credit in plain words

Think of each account as a box. Debit means value goes into a box you own or a cost you paid (cash, stock, expenses, customers who owe you). Credit means where it came from (sales, suppliers you owe, your capital). Both sides always add up to the same amount.

CHAPTER 14

Stock control

  • FEFO: the earliest-expiry batch goes first. Changing it needs permission and a reason.
  • Reorder: average daily sale × supplier lead time + safety stock = reorder point. Suggested order = maximum − (in hand + on order − reserved).
  • Counts: count the shelf, compare, post the difference with a reason.
  • Transfers: out of one branch, in transit, into another. Between different GSTINs it is a taxable sale with a tax invoice.
  • Expiry status: a batch expires on the last day of its printed month. "11/2026" means 30 Nov 2026.
  • Average cost: after each purchase, cost = (old stock × old cost + new units × new cost) ÷ all units.

CHAPTER 19

Test yourself

Does MRP include GST?

Yes. At the counter MENO divides by (1 + GST %) to find the taxable value.

Does PTR include GST?

No. GST is added on top of PTR on a wholesale bill.

A customer in the same state buys medicine with 5 % GST. Which taxes?

CGST 2.5 % + SGST 2.5 %. For another state it would be IGST 5 %.

Which batch does MENO sell first?

The one that expires first (FEFO).

Can you sell a batch that expired yesterday?

No. MENO blocks it.

What does Schedule H1 need at the counter?

A prescription, and a register entry with prescriber, patient, medicine and quantity, kept 3 years.

You entered a bill with a mistake and saved it. Can you edit it?

No. Make a credit note, debit note or reversal. Saved bills never change.

10 strips + 1 free. How much does stock go up?

11 strips.

What is landed cost?

What one unit really cost after discounts, free goods and freight.

A strip of 15 tablets has MRP ₹33.60. Price of one tablet?

₹2.24.

Customer pays ₹650; bills of ₹500 and ₹300 are open. What happens?

₹500 clears the older bill, ₹150 goes to the second; ₹150 is still due on it.

Who files GST returns?

You or your accountant on the GST portal. MENO prepares and exports the data.

When is e-invoice needed?

When yearly turnover is above ₹5 crore.

What is a credit note?

A document that reduces what a party owes, for example after a return.

Why are tax rates settings, not code?

Because laws change. A dated setting changes new bills without touching old ones or the software.
